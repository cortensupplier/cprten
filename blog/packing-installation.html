Packing, storage and installation: protect the agreed finish

The material specification continues through dispatch and site handling. Plan how each piece arrives, is identified and is stored.

SHANDONG DAZHI / BUYER RESOURCESUpdated 17 September 20263 min read

Match packing to the product

Raw plate, cut blanks and pre-weathered architectural panels have different handling priorities. State the piece dimensions, finish expectations and visible faces before discussing protection. Agree package weights and lifting access with the receiving team. For finished panels, define any separators and surface protection with the supplier so the packing arrangement is compatible with the agreed finish.

Make receiving straightforward

Use consistent part numbers on the drawing, packing list and piece labels. Confirm how heat or batch identification is retained when a plate is cut into several items. Keep the required documents accessible and separate from easily damaged exterior labels. At receipt, check the package condition and material references, and record any discrepancy before the parts are moved into general storage.

Avoid unmanaged moisture exposure

Standing water, trapped moisture and contaminated storage conditions can affect surfaces. Agree storage instructions for the supplied finish, including separation from the ground and how water can drain and the material can dry. Do not assume that an architectural pre-weathered panel can be stacked wet without affecting its appearance. Follow the actual supplier’s handling guidance for any coating or temporary protection.

Coordinate the installation sequence

Review lifting, edge protection, visible-face orientation and the order in which panels will be installed. Protect neighboring finishes from construction runoff and surface contamination. Confirm how labels and temporary protection are removed without damaging the accepted finish. Include the final destination, unloading constraints and any special packing requirements in your inquiry. The quotation should identify the agreed packing scope and delivery basis rather than leave them to assumption.

Bundle layout, loading and shipping marks

Separate the packing drawing from the material schedule. Show bundle contents, separators, edge protection, component marks and the intended unloading sequence. Confirm transport supports and blocking with the logistics provider for the actual cargo and route; a container designation alone does not establish an acceptable load plan.

Use shipping marks that match the packing list and material register. Record gross and net mass as required by the shipment, identify the receiving contractor, and keep labels readable after wrapping. Photograph protected edges and bundle labels before dispatch and note damage or wet packaging at each transfer.

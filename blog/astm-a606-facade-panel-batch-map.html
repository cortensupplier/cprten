ASTM A606 Facade Batch Mapping: Keep Panel Identity Across Elevations

Plan panel marks, sheet batches and installation locations so an A606 facade can be received and assembled consistently.

SHANDONG DAZHI10 November 2026

Colour variation is a layout issue too

Weathering develops with exposure and time, so two A606 panels may look different when delivered or after installation. A finish sample establishes a review reference but cannot make every panel age at the same rate. For an architectural elevation, the practical control is to map where each panel comes from and where it will be installed.

Give each panel a location

Use an elevation, grid line and orientation arrow in the panel schedule. The label on the pack should match the mark on the installation drawing. For mirrored or rotated panels, include a visible-face indication. This avoids accidental reversal, which can put fixing slots or drainage folds on the wrong side.

Link sheets to the record

Where the purchase specification requires traceability, record the source sheet or coil and heat for each panel group. Keep any supplier batch reference distinct from the architectural finish batch. The material certificate supports identity, while the approved sample and drawing govern appearance and geometry.

Plan replacement before it is needed

Reserve a clear procedure for a damaged panel: determine its mark, required type, thickness, surface condition and current drawing revision. A replacement produced months later may begin with a different colour. The design team should decide whether panels are blended, pre-exposed or installed as a deliberate contrast.

Close the loop at handover

Keep the final panel map with the inspection and maintenance documents. It helps the owner locate a part without guessing from photographs. The image shows generic weathered sheet cladding and does not identify an A606 project. For material procurement, see folded facade cassettes.

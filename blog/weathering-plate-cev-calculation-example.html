Carbon Equivalent for Weathering Plate: A CEV Calculation Example

Calculate an illustrative IIW carbon equivalent from stated chemistry and keep it separate from welding procedure approval.

SHANDONG DAZHI21 November 2026

An IIW carbon equivalent calculation combines selected chemical contents into one number for the agreed assessment method. It is useful only when the formula and input basis are explicit. A calculated CEV does not on its own set the welding procedure or establish an acceptable preheat temperature.

State the formula and use consistent inputs

The IIW CEV expression is C + Mn/6 + (Cr + Mo + V)/5 + (Ni + Cu)/15. Enter each element as its numerical mass percentage: a report value of 0.12 percent carbon is entered as 0.12. Identify whether the inputs are actual reported chemistry or specification limits. Do not mix values from unrelated heats to produce a number for a supplied plate.

Work through a transparent illustrative example

Assume C = 0.12, Mn = 1.20, Cr = 0.50, Mo = 0.02, V = 0.03, Ni = 0.25 and Cu = 0.30, all in mass percent. The terms are 0.12, 0.20, 0.11 and approximately 0.0367. Their sum is approximately 0.4667 percent, or 0.467 percent when rounded to three decimal places. These are invented calculation inputs, not a grade specification or a heat certificate.

Keep CEV distinct from CET and carbon-footprint data

CEV and CET are different formula conventions and should not be interchanged without reviewing the assessment method. Carbon equivalent is also unrelated to a product carbon footprint reported as greenhouse-gas emissions. Label the calculated quantity, units and method clearly, especially when procurement documents contain several figures described informally as carbon values.

Pass the calculation to the procedure review

The welding assessment also needs the actual material condition, joint, thickness, restraint, consumables, hydrogen control and applicable procedure requirements. Ask the responsible welding specialist to determine how the selected method uses the chemistry. If a certificate omits an input needed for the calculation, request an accepted basis rather than silently assuming zero. Retain the source report and rounding convention with the result.

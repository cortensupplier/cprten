Weathering Plate Rolling Direction: Keep It Through Cutting

Keep plate rolling direction connected to cut blanks, bend references and inspection drawings when orientation matters to the project.

SHANDONG DAZHI23 November 2026

Preserve rolling direction before nesting and cutting whenever the approved fabrication or inspection requirements depend on it. Identify the direction from accepted producer information and carry it onto the cut-piece register. The longest side of a cut blank is not reliable evidence of the original rolling direction.

Define which direction the drawing means

Distinguish the original plate rolling direction, the bend line, the component length and any test orientation. Give each reference a clear label. A note saying longitudinal can be ambiguous if the workshop reads it against the finished part while the material report uses the parent plate. Add a small orientation sketch to resolve that ambiguity before nesting.

Make orientation a nesting constraint

Show whether rotation of a blank is unrestricted or subject to the approved direction requirement. Ask the processor to confirm how that constraint affects the proposed arrangement and material use. Do not approve a rotated part merely because the outline still fits the plate. Review any proposed change through the same drawing process that established the original orientation.

Transfer the reference to individual pieces

Use an agreed marking or labelling method that preserves the direction and the part identity without conflicting with visible-face requirements. Connect the mark to the parent plate and cutting layout. Keep a copy of that layout after the remnants are separated. A photograph of an unmarked rectangular piece usually cannot reconstruct its original orientation.

Check the final part against the correct reference

At first-piece inspection, compare the orientation mark with the accepted component drawing and any forming setup. If the direction has been lost, record it as an unresolved information issue rather than recreating an arrow from the apparent grain or surface colour. The project reviewer can then determine what evidence or disposition is appropriate before further processing.

Orientation information to retain

Review itemBasis or example
Parent referenceProducer identity and accepted rolling-direction evidence
DrawingRelationship to bend lines and part datums
NestingPermitted rotations and layout revision
Cut piecePart mark, direction and transfer record

Treat required orientation as controlled fabrication information from the parent plate to the finished blank.

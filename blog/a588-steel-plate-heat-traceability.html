A588 Steel Plate Heat Traceability After Cutting

A traceability route from mill plate to A588 blanks, packed bundles and receiving records.

SHANDONG DAZHI2 November 2026

A traceability route from mill plate to A588 blanks, packed bundles and receiving records.

Begin with a verified parent identity

At the first handling stage, match the plate marking and certificate heat number to the purchasing line. Record the parent plate dimensions and an internal job identifier. Do this before cutting; an unmarked stack of similar-looking weathering plates cannot be reliably sorted later by surface color.

Plan the mark transfer

Number the child pieces on a cutting map. Decide whether each will be paint-marked, tagged, stamped where permitted, or carried within a controlled labeled bundle. The chosen method must remain readable after any blast cleaning, beveling or coating. Keep the transfer register with the work order.

Use one reconciliation table

A useful register includes parent heat, parent plate mark, child part number, quantity, processing step, package number and certificate reference. Reconcile total child pieces with the cut plan, rejected pieces and remnants. This is more informative than attaching a certificate to a shipment without showing which item it covers.

Check the handover

The receiver should be able to trace a selected part back to its certificate without opening every bundle. Ask for a sample trace during pre-shipment review and at delivery. If the chain breaks, quarantine the affected pieces for resolution rather than assigning a likely heat from nearby material.

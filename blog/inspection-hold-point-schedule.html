Setting Inspection Hold Points for Steel Fabrication

Place inspection decisions before the manufacturing operations that would conceal the relevant evidence.

SHANDONG DAZHI28 October 2026

Choose the stage for the decision

A hold point should have a clear purpose: a decision or observation must occur before the next operation proceeds. Locate it against the actual manufacturing route. Material identity may need review before cutting, while an inaccessible interface may need inspection before assembly closes it. Adding an inspection to the end of the order cannot recover evidence that is no longer visible.

State release authority and notice

Name the party who can release the hold, the required notice and the records they need. Distinguish a mandatory stop from a witness opportunity or a document review. If attendance is not possible, agree the response through the purchase requirements rather than assuming that an unanswered email automatically permits production to continue.

Keep evidence with the release

The release record should identify the relevant material or parts, inspection stage, drawing revision and outcome. Describe any accepted condition or outstanding action. Send that information to production and include it in the shipment records where required. A signed schedule with no link to the inspected components is much less useful when the buyer later asks what was actually checked.

A practical record structure

RecordInformation to agree
Material reviewBefore cutting removes parent marks
Hidden interfaceBefore final closure
First pieceBefore repeating a batch
Dispatch releaseBefore the agreed shipping stage

Before closing the review

  • Choose stages from the real production route.
  • Name the release authority.
  • Keep evidence linked to the affected pieces.

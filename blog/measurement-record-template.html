A Measurement Record for Fabricated Steel Parts

Record the feature, setup and result so dimensional checks can be reviewed after delivery.

SHANDONG DAZHI28 October 2026

Identify the feature before recording a number

A dimensional report needs to show what was measured. Reference the part mark, drawing revision, feature and datum. Record the required value or tolerance beside the result. A page of numbers with no feature labels cannot reliably distinguish an overall length from a hole coordinate or show whether a measurement belongs to the current drawing.

Describe the measurement setup

State the inspection stage and relevant support or restraint condition. Identify the instrument used and the equipment records required by the project. For surfaces or geometry sensitive to setup, include a sketch or photograph that makes the method repeatable. Use the same measurement basis agreed for acceptance instead of changing the setup to obtain a preferred result.

Keep results and decisions separate

Report observed values clearly and identify who evaluates acceptance. If a feature needs correction or review, retain the original observation and link the later re-check to it. A summary marked accepted should remain traceable to the underlying measurements. Deliver the required report with the part register so the receiving team can locate the inspected feature without guessing.

A practical record structure

RecordInformation to agree
Feature referenceDrawing datum and dimension identifier
SetupSupport and inspection stage
EquipmentInstrument identity
ResultObserved value and disposition

Before closing the review

  • Use feature identifiers from the drawing.
  • Document the inspection setup.
  • Keep re-checks connected to the initial result.

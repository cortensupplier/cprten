Price per Tonne or per Piece? Compare the Finished Plate Scope

Normalise a hypothetical material-and-cutting quotation by accepted finished quantity and included services.

SHANDONG DAZHI21 November 2026

A material rate per tonne and a price per finished piece can only be compared after their scopes and mass bases are aligned. Use the accepted number of components as one common denominator and list the included operations. A lower raw-material rate can still produce a higher cost per usable part.

Define one output for both offers

Use the same drawing revision, grade, delivered condition, piece quantity and dimensional acceptance. Ask whether the price includes parent stock, cutting, holes, edge finishing, documents and packaging. Keep delivery and taxes on a consistent comparison basis where applicable. A price for rough blanks is not directly comparable with a price for finished components.

Follow a hypothetical calculation

Suppose 20 accepted components each have a theoretical net mass of 12 kg, while the proposed nesting requires 0.300 tonnes of allocated parent stock. An illustrative material rate of 900 currency units per tonne gives 270 currency units of material cost. Add assumed cutting of 120 and packing of 30: the stated total is 420, or 21 per finished piece. These figures are arithmetic assumptions, not current market prices or an offer from SHANDONG DAZHI.

Show where net-mass pricing changes the comparison

The 20 parts contain only 0.240 tonnes of theoretical net material. Multiplying that net mass by the same illustrative rate gives 216, which leaves a 54-unit difference from the allocated-stock basis before processing. Establish how remnants, scrap and nesting charges are treated instead of assuming either basis is automatically included. Record any ownership or credit arrangement explicitly.

Carry exclusions into the final comparison

List the services not included and obtain their confirmed cost or keep them unresolved. Divide by the same accepted quantity only after the relevant scope is aligned. For changes in quantity or drawing geometry, request a revised nesting and processing basis; the original piece rate may no longer represent the same production task. Keep the numerical worksheet with the quote issue used for the decision.

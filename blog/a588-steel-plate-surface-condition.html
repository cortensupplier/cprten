A588 Steel Plate Surface Condition at Delivery: What to Accept

Separate delivery surface condition from eventual patina when specifying and inspecting A588 plate.

SHANDONG DAZHI3 November 2026

Separate delivery surface condition from eventual patina when specifying and inspecting A588 plate.

Name the delivered condition

A588 is a material specification; it does not promise a particular rust color at dispatch. State whether the order is for as-rolled plate, cleaned plate or a separately agreed surface treatment. If a visible face matters, describe the acceptance method, sample and any permitted variation.

Distinguish appearance from damage

A plate may show different tones after storage or transit. Inspect for agreed surface defects, mechanical damage and edge condition using the purchase terms. Do not use color alone to identify the heat or grade. Link any photographed exception to the piece and package mark.

Consider processing order

Cutting, drilling, blasting and welding can change the appearance of a previously weathered face. Decide whether the finish is reviewed before or after fabrication and which areas are allowed to be retouched. A small sample may help align expectations but should match the production route.

Document the receiving decision

Keep a dated record of supplied condition, samples, rejected areas and approved remedies. The final patina develops in service according to exposure and maintenance; it should not be promised as an identical color across every new plate.

FINISH SAMPLE APPROVAL

Approve a finish sample without promising a fixed colour

A sample can align expectations at delivery, but the future patina will still respond to exposure and drainage.

SHANDONG DAZHI / BUYER GUIDEPublished 21 October 20262 min read

Agree what the sample represents

A photograph is useful for discussing intent, but lighting, screen settings and wetness can change the apparent colour. For visible architectural work, a physical sample can record the agreed initial appearance. Label it with the base material, preparation method, orientation, date and approval reference.

Ask whether the sample demonstrates colour only or also texture, weld appearance, cut edges and protective handling. A small flat coupon may not represent a folded panel with joints and fasteners.

Document acceptable variation

Approval itemRecord
Base gradeStandard, grade and condition under the finish
ViewingDry or wet state, daylight conditions and viewing distance
FacesWhich surfaces and cut edges are visible
VariationRange of tone and texture acceptable at delivery
PackagingHow contact marks and moisture are controlled in transit

Inspect a representative mock-up after the same fabrication sequence intended for the project. If panels are welded, bent or perforated, the approval should show those features. Keep the signed sample record with the drawing revision and purchase order.

Plan for change after installation

  • Explain that the approved sample describes initial delivery, not a permanent colour.
  • Detail water paths and protection of adjacent finishes.
  • Set the process for reviewing variations or damaged panels.
  • Confirm who approves the sample and who accepts installed work.

See pre-weathered plate and the runoff guide. A finish brief should sit alongside the base steel specification, not replace it.

This guide is general buying information. The current material standard, project drawing and agreed purchase documents control technical acceptance and supply.

Agree preparation as well as colour

A finish sample should record the starting surface, preparation process, treatment and date. Shot blasting, mill scale removal and an established patina can produce different starting conditions. State the intended surface profile and cleanliness through the agreed treatment specification; do not infer these from a small photograph.

Approve the sample under stated viewing and wetness conditions and keep a labelled control piece. If a later batch uses another preparation or treatment process, review a new sample rather than assuming it will reproduce the first result. Keep the sample approval separate from the material certificate and dimensional inspection.

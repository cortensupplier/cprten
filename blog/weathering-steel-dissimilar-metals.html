Weathering Steel Interfaces with Other Metals and Coatings

Review wet contact, isolation components, coatings and inspection access where weathering steel meets other materials.

SHANDONG DAZHI / BUYER RESOURCESUpdated 27 October 20264 min read

Review the contact and its environment together

A connection between weathering steel and another metal needs an interface review, especially where moisture can remain between the materials. Listing the metal names is only the first step. The designer also needs the contact geometry, wetting conditions, exposed areas and the path by which water leaves the joint.

Show stainless, galvanized, aluminium and painted components explicitly on the assembly drawing. Ask the responsible designer to select a compatible arrangement for the actual environment. A familiar fastener material or a single washer does not automatically establish that the whole interface is suitable.

Specify the complete isolation arrangement

Where isolation is required, identify every intended component, including pads, sleeves, washers and separation at edges. A washer can separate a bolt head while the bolt shank still contacts the hole. Draw the intended contact points so purchasing, fabrication and installation use the same arrangement.

Review the isolator material for compression, exposure and the conditions of the connection. Its dimensions and durability belong in the approved detail. Avoid improvised packing that changes alignment, traps water or interferes with the designed fastener engagement.

Treat primers as an agreed coating system

A primer or local coating should be selected for the prepared substrate, service exposure and intended topcoat. Identify coated zones, bare weathering zones and the transition between them. Masking lines, underside coverage and repair of handling damage need to be defined before application.

Confirm the coating manufacturer instructions, surface preparation and compatibility of all layers through the project coating specification. Do not assume any primer can be applied over an established patina or that coating a contact patch replaces a designed isolation system. Record the approved preparation and inspection requirements.

Inspect what the assembled joint will conceal

Before final assembly, photograph the contact surfaces, isolators and coated areas that will become inaccessible. Check that drainage has not been blocked by sealant, paint buildup or a misplaced pad. Keep the records linked to the connection or panel mark.

Provide access for periodic checks of moisture traps, coating breakdown and corrosion at interfaces. When a substitution is proposed, review the complete assembly rather than approving one replacement material from its trade name alone.

Before releasing the order

  • List every metal and coating at the interface.
  • Show wetting and drainage conditions in section.
  • Detail isolation around the full connection where required.
  • Record hidden surfaces before closure and define inspection access.

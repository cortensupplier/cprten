Flatness and cut tolerances are different checks

A mill certificate can identify a grade without proving that a cut panel meets an architectural drawing's final dimensions.

SHANDONG DAZHI / BUYER GUIDEPublished 21 October 20262 min read

Two stages, two sets of acceptance rules

Mill supply is ordered to a product standard and its dimensional requirements. Cutting, leveling, bending and drilling create a second acceptance stage for the finished part. An order that says only “standard tolerances” can leave the buyer and processor measuring different things.

For a flat panel, mark the inspection face, support condition and measuring method. State whether flatness is assessed on the unrestrained sheet or after it is fixed to a subframe. For a cut blank, identify the datum and whether overall length, diagonal difference or edge squareness is important.

Put each tolerance next to the feature

FeaturePossible acceptance detail
ThicknessApplicable base product standard and inspection point
FlatnessFace, support setup, span and measurement method
OutlineFinished width, length, corner and diagonal requirements
Cut edgeBurr, taper, roughness or finishing where relevant
HolesDatum, size and position relative to mounting system

Critical architectural fit or automated assembly may justify a first-piece approval before the entire order is processed. If the drawing uses unusually tight values, check fabrication feasibility and inspection cost before fixing a delivery date.

Keep the paperwork connected

  • Quote the base material standard and finished-part drawing revision.
  • Separate mill inspection records from part-dimensional records.
  • Specify how nonconforming parts will be identified and reviewed.
  • Agree packaging that preserves the accepted shape.

Check the measured condition and site release

Define thickness measurement locations and the applicable dimensional standard. For flatness and straightness, state the support condition, reference span, measuring method and inspection stage. A plate measured while constrained in an assembly is not the same acceptance check as a loose delivered blank.

Where cut sizes depend on site dimensions, identify the survey datum, unit system, responsible person and drawing release date. Confirm proposed straightening with the fabricator before work; the method must be appropriate to the material and accepted fabrication procedure. Record finished dimensions after any approved correction.

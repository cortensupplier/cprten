Weathering Plate Weight with Cutouts: A Worked Calculation

Calculate an example annular plate with bolt holes and distinguish net geometry from parent-stock consumption.

SHANDONG DAZHI21 November 2026

For a flat plate of uniform thickness, estimate mass from the net plan area, thickness and an agreed nominal density. Subtract openings once and keep units consistent. The resulting component mass differs from the mass of stock purchased to cut it and from the gross mass of its shipping package.

Define the example geometry

Consider a hypothetical flat ring with an outside diameter of 1,000 mm, an inside diameter of 400 mm, eight separate 22 mm diameter holes and a thickness of 10 mm. The holes lie entirely within the remaining material and do not overlap each other. Use a nominal density of 7,850 kg per cubic metre. These dimensions describe a calculation example, not an available stocked part.

Subtract the openings before applying thickness

The annular area is pi multiplied by (1,000 squared minus 400 squared), divided by four: approximately 659,734.46 square millimetres. The eight holes remove approximately 3,041.06 square millimetres. Net area is therefore approximately 656,693.40 square millimetres. Multiplying that area by 10 mm and by 0.00000785 gives an estimated net mass of 51.55 kg.

Resolve complex geometry in a controlled drawing

For intersecting openings, a hole near an edge or a nonuniform profile, avoid subtracting the same region twice. Use the accepted drawing or a checked CAD area or volume when the shape becomes difficult to describe. Confirm model units, suppressed features and the revision used. Attached welds, studs or other parts need separate treatment if the required quantity is assembly mass.

Keep three different quantities on the quotation

The finished net component, the parent stock allocated to cutting and the packed shipment answer different questions. Nesting, kerf and unusable remnants can increase material consumption without increasing net part mass. Separators and packaging add to transport mass. Label the estimate accordingly and obtain the agreed final shipping or invoicing basis rather than using theoretical net geometry for every purpose.

A588 Steel Plate vs Corten B: Specify the Actual Purchase Grade

An A588 and Corten B buying comparison focused on contract designation, grade evidence and substitution approval.

SHANDONG DAZHI4 November 2026

An A588 and Corten B buying comparison focused on contract designation, grade evidence and substitution approval.

Use a contractual designation

A588 is an ASTM material specification; “Corten B” is often used commercially as a weathering steel name. The two labels should not be treated as automatically interchangeable on a purchase order. Ask the project engineer to state the permitted specification, grade and edition.

Compare the offered material

Request the supplier's exact standard designation, product form, thickness and heat certificate. Review the chemistry, mechanical requirements and any additional project tests against the approved design. Similar outdoor appearance or a supplier's marketing phrase does not establish equivalence.

Consider downstream approvals

Check whether drawings, welding procedures, inspection plans or coating decisions name a particular material. A substitution may affect documents beyond the plate line. Keep alternative offers separate until the responsible designer approves a change.

Close the commercial record

The final order should name the accepted designation and certificate requirements. If the project uses a trade name in correspondence, attach the precise material line so the mill, fabricator and receiving inspector evaluate the same product.

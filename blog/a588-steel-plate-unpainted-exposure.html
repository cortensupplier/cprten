Can A588 Steel Plate Remain Unpainted at This Site?

A project review of drying, salt, crevices and runoff before approving bare A588 plate for outdoor use.

SHANDONG DAZHI2 November 2026

A project review of drying, salt, crevices and runoff before approving bare A588 plate for outdoor use.

Begin with the actual exposure

A588 steel plate has atmospheric corrosion resistance, but an unpainted application needs suitable exposure. Document whether the location dries between wetting events, receives salt spray or deicing salts, or traps persistent moisture. Review the precise location of each plate, not just the regional climate.

Map water pathways

Mark horizontal ledges, overlapping joints, drainage holes and contact with concrete or paving. Water retained at a lap or enclosed pocket can behave differently from an open vertical face. Plan where early rust-colored runoff will go and which adjacent surfaces need protection.

Obtain a project decision

The responsible designer should decide whether each zone is left bare, protected by a coating system or redesigned for drainage. A steel supplier can supply the specified material and discuss processing, but a material designation alone cannot approve a service environment.

Make the decision inspectable

Add the exposure zone, intended finish and maintenance access to the drawing or schedule. A future inspector should be able to distinguish an approved bare surface from a missed coating operation. Revisit the decision if site details or deicing practices change.

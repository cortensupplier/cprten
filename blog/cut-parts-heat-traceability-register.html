After Plate Cutting: Keep Heat Identity Connected to Each Required Part

A certificate may describe the parent material while the customer receives many smaller pieces. Agree the required identification chain before cutting begins.

SHANDONG DAZHI / BUYER GUIDEPublished 22 October 20262 min read

Original concept illustration, not to scale. The approved drawing defines actual geometry and acceptance.

Define the level of identification the order requires

Material identification and full piece-by-piece traceability are not automatically the same requirement. The project specification should establish what must be retained and demonstrated. Ask the buyer to state the required relationship between the delivered parts, their material batches and the inspection documents.

Build that requirement into the order before processing. It is harder to reconstruct an undocumented link after parts have been separated, mixed or surface-treated.

Connect the nesting sheet to the part register

The shop record should distinguish parent material identity from the finished part mark. When a plate is nested into multiple shapes, record the relationship in the system required by the project. Keep repeated part marks from different parent material identifiable to the necessary level.

Specify how marking may be transferred without damaging an appearance-critical face or violating the drawing. Labels, durable marks and location rules need agreement for the actual finishing and packing route. No single marking method suits every project.

Include remnants and changes in the handover logic

A remnant re-entering production needs the relevant identity and material information. A clear offcut shape alone does not establish its grade or heat. Record the controls applied when usable remnants are separated from scrap.

At dispatch, align the packing list, part schedule and required material records. If the customer expects a particular register or certificate index, agree its format early and confirm who supplies it. A pile of unindexed documents can leave the same practical uncertainty as a missing record.

Before releasing the order

  • State the identification level in the RFQ.
  • Agree transfer controls before cutting.
  • Keep remnants within the same required record system.
  • Match delivered parts to the agreed document register.

Purchase the identification process your project requires, then retain the evidence that the agreed process was followed.

Separate batches and choose a marking method

Keep grades, heats, thicknesses and drawing revisions distinct in the part register. Record how each child part relates to the original plate when a heat is split across cutting nests or packing bundles. A batch label that only names the product family cannot resolve which certificate belongs to an individual required part.

Agree marking positions, method and durability with the purchaser. Laser marking, paint marking, tags and stamps have different visibility and surface implications; the selected method must satisfy the project requirement. Verify marks after cutting and before packing, and keep the register available to the receiving inspector.

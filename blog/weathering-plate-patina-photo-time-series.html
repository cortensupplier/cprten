How to Record Weathering Plate Patina Changes with Comparable Photos

Build a dated observation series with controlled viewing conditions instead of treating unrelated photographs as a performance test.

SHANDONG DAZHI21 November 2026

A useful patina photo record follows identified material over time and states the observation conditions. It can support an appearance discussion, but it does not establish a universal weathering time or prove corrosion performance. The record should make differences in exposure and photography visible to the reviewer.

Keep one specimen identity through the series

Assign an identifier to each observed plate or sample and record the material information actually available. Note the initial surface preparation, treatment and date placed in the observation location. If a panel is replaced or moved, record the change rather than continuing the same series as though nothing happened. Retain the original files with the identifier and capture date.

Make the photographs reasonably comparable

Use the same face, approximate viewpoint, distance and lighting arrangement where practical. Include a scale and a consistent colour reference if appearance comparison matters. Record whether the surface is wet or dry, because that condition can change how it looks in a photograph. Avoid processing different images with unrelated filters and then interpreting the resulting colour difference as material behaviour.

Record exposure beside the image

Note the site location at an appropriate project level, orientation, shelter, nearby runoff and any known cleaning or treatment. Include the observation date and the relevant weather or wetness condition. Photographs taken under different exposures do not form a controlled comparison simply because their filenames contain the same day number. Record the observations you have without inventing missing environmental measurements.

Define the decision the series can support

Use the record to discuss starting appearance, variation or an agreed review stage. If the project needs evidence about thickness loss, corrosion rate or another performance property, specify an appropriate separate investigation with the responsible specialist. Publish a time series only when the underlying observations exist. An illustrative schedule or a collection of supplied stock photos must remain clearly distinguished from actual dated field results.

Minimum observation record

Review itemBasis or example
SpecimenIdentifier and known material preparation
CaptureDate, viewpoint, lighting and wet or dry condition
ExposureOrientation, shelter and relevant changes
ObservationAppearance note and associated original photograph

A credible time series needs traceable observations; the photographs alone do not establish a performance claim.

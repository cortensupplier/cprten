BUYER GUIDE

Tracking Corrections to a Fabricated Steel Part

Keep the finding, proposed remedy, approval and re-inspection connected in one record.

SHANDONG DAZHI28 October 2026

Describe the observed difference

Identify the part, drawing revision and feature involved. Record what was observed and the requirement used for comparison. Include marked photographs or measurements where useful. A statement such as part wrong is difficult to act on; a located feature and an agreed requirement let the supplier investigate without assuming that every piece in the batch has the same issue.

Obtain a disposition before changing the part

Ask for the proposed action and the responsible approval. Possible outcomes depend on the project and may include correction, replacement or acceptance through the required concession process. Do not treat a workshop repair suggestion as approval to change an engineered feature. Keep responsibility for technical decisions with the authorised project parties.

Verify the action and close the record

Identify what was changed, which pieces were affected and how the result was re-checked. Preserve the original finding alongside the closing evidence. If replacement material is supplied, update its identity and document links in the order register. Closing one report should not silently remove another outstanding requirement, such as missing certificates or an uncompleted quantity balance.

A practical record structure

RecordInformation to agree
FindingPart, feature and requirement
ProposalCorrection or replacement scope
ApprovalAuthorised disposition
ClosureRe-inspection and remaining actions

Before closing the review

  • Describe the feature and the evidence.
  • Approve the remedy before work.
  • Close with a traceable re-check.

Laser, plasma or waterjet: choose by the part

The cutting route should follow the plate thickness, detail size, edge acceptance and later forming or welding work.

SHANDONG DAZHI / BUYER GUIDEPublished 21 October 20262 min read

Send the drawing before naming a machine

A cut profile can often be made by more than one process. Laser, plasma and waterjet methods have different practical ranges and edge characteristics; actual capability depends on the processor's equipment and the plate supplied. Do not select a method from a photograph or a generic thickness chart alone.

Mark small slots, internal corners, hole sizes and the faces that will remain visible. Also identify whether edges will be welded, machined, ground or left exposed. The required finished edge can matter more than the raw cutting speed.

Questions for each route

RouteClarify with the processor
LaserDetail size, plate range, edge appearance and heat effects
PlasmaThickness capacity, kerf and secondary edge finishing
WaterjetEdge taper, surface texture, abrasive cleanup and handling

Any process may require allowance for kerf, distortion or later finishing. If parts are to be bent, the drawing should locate the bend relative to the cut edge and state its acceptance criteria. For exposed architectural work, a trial piece can resolve appearance questions early.

Write a process-neutral RFQ when appropriate

  • Specify material grade, thickness, drawing revision and quantity.
  • Mark critical holes, slots, radii and visible edges.
  • State required edge condition and any weld preparation.
  • Allow the supplier to propose the feasible cutting route and finishing sequence.

See weathering steel plate products and the guide to finished-part tolerances. The approved drawing, not the process label, determines whether the part is acceptable.

This guide is general buying information. The current material standard, project drawing and agreed purchase documents control technical acceptance and supply.

Accept the cut edge, not just the process name

Describe edge taper, burr or dross removal, corner features and any visible-face requirements on the part drawing. A laser-cut part can still need finishing; a sheared sheet has a different edge condition from a thermal cut. Agree the acceptance sample or inspection method against the actual thickness and geometry.

Provide clean closed CAD outlines, correct units and a controlled revision. CNC describes machine control, not proof that a routing operation is suitable for every steel plate. Review routing, machining, shear cutting or a thermal process with the processor and specify the finished result. Any cut-edge coating or sealing must follow the approved finish system, rather than being assumed necessary for every bare weathering-steel edge.

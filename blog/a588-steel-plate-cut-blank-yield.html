Cutting A588 Steel Plate Blanks: Yield, Marks and Remnants

Avoid mismatched A588 cut-blank quotes by defining nesting, piece identity and remnant ownership.

Avoid mismatched A588 cut-blank quotes by defining nesting, piece identity and remnant ownership.

Give the fabricator a part map

Provide a drawing for each A588 steel plate blank with its finished geometry, cut tolerance and orientation mark where one applies. If the part will be machined or beveled later, identify the machining allowance separately. A single gross area figure cannot tell the cutting shop how to preserve these details.

Price the same yield basis

Ask whether the quotation counts gross purchased plate, net shipped blanks, or both. Include planned kerf, crop ends and rejection allowance in the commercial explanation. This is especially useful when comparing a stock plate offer with a cut-to-size offer that appears cheaper per kilogram.

Keep heat identity through cutting

Agree how the original heat number will be transferred or mapped to every blank before the parent plate is separated. If small pieces cannot carry a legible mark, require a controlled bundle or part map. Traceability should survive the cutting sequence, not be reconstructed from memory at dispatch.

Agree what happens to offcuts

State whether usable remnants are returned, retained or credited, and whether their heat identity and dimensions must be recorded. This turns a vague “best yield” promise into an auditable material plan.
